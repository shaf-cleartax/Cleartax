**01  What was actually announced**

On 16 July 2026, Germany's Federal Ministry of Finance and Federal Ministry of Justice jointly published an Action Plan against Tax and Financial Crime containing 26 measures. There is no draft legislation yet, no implementation timetable, no reporting format and no published technical architecture. This is direction, not law, and direction is exactly what a CFO plans against.

Four measures matter most for VAT:

* **A VAT reporting system (Umsatzsteuermeldesystem).** Transactions reported individually and promptly, so carousel fraud surfaces faster.

* **A joint data analysis centre with the Länder.** A central tax data platform, with federal and state authorities given shared access.

* **AI-supported analysis.** Tools to identify suspicious patterns across very large volumes of financial data.

* **A Joint Centre Against Tax and Financial Crime.** Housed in the customs authority, pulling federal and state investigators into one room.

**02  Why this lands harder in Germany than anywhere else**

Tax administration in Germany sits with the 16 federal states. That fragmentation is precisely why Germany has never had the single national VAT database that Italy or Hungary already operate and missing-trader and carousel fraud has historically lived in the seams between authorities.

Removing the seams is the real news. AI is the headline; the central database is the story. No model can trace a missing-trader chain that is split across sixteen separate systems.

Germany's phased B2B e-invoicing mandate began in 2025 and reaches full scope on 1 January 2028\. Layer one is already being built inside your ERP. The Action Plan simply tells you what is going to be built on top of it — and AI is not replacing conventional VAT control here. It is the intelligence layer sitting above a much broader digital compliance architecture.

**04  What changes for your finance function**

**Assessment moves from the period to the transaction.** The EU's ViDA digital reporting requirements apply mainly to intra-EU B2B from 1 July 2030\. Germany's wording — individual, prompt reporting — reads broader, and it has not confirmed whether domestic transactions are in scope. Plan for domestic.

**Your data stays discoverable for 15 years.** Retention on accounting records extends from 8 years to 15\. Businesses headquartered outside the EU would additionally be required to hold tax-relevant data on mirror servers located in Germany.

**The safety net is being removed.** Voluntary disclosure in its current form — immunity from prosecution for coming forward before detection — is proposed for abolition. Quietly correcting a position after the fact stops being a viable strategy.

**Getting it wrong gets more expensive, and more public.** Corporate-level sanctions for tax evasion, a public register of companies sanctioned for serious tax offences, and prison sentences of up to 15 years for serious organised tax crime. This is reputational exposure, not only financial.

**Getting it right gets cheaper.** Expanded risk-oriented federal audit is explicitly framed as relief for businesses that follow the rules. Better data means clean taxpayers get looked at less often. That is the trade the whole plan rests on: accuracy is rewarded, drift is punished.

**05  The honest timeline**

**What is settled:** the e-invoicing mandate reaches full scope on 1 January 2028, and the European direction of travel is no longer in question. Belgium mandated structured domestic B2B e-invoicing from January 2026 and is expected to add real-time transactional reporting from 2028\. France begins its mandate on a phased basis from September 2026, combining e-invoicing, e-reporting and an accredited-platform ecosystem in a single programme, with large enterprises and ETI issuing from that date and SMEs following from September 2027\.

**What is not settled:** whether Germany's reporting system covers domestic transactions, when it starts, in what format, on what architecture — and the fact that no draft law exists yet.

The planning answer does not depend on resolving any of that. Every version of this future asks the same thing of you: invoice-level data that agrees with your ledger, every day, before anyone else looks at it.

**06  What to do in the next two quarters**

1. **Assume transaction-level.** Test whether your ERP can produce a clean, invoice-level VAT position on demand — not just a monthly aggregate.

2. **Find your gaps before the platform does.** Where do issued e-invoices, GL entries and filed returns disagree today? That delta is your future notice list.

3. **Start the retention conversation now.** Fifteen years and German data residency are archive and infrastructure decisions with long lead times — IT and legal, not tax alone.

4. **Retire the quiet fix.** If any process silently assumes voluntary disclosure as a backstop, rebuild it around detection before filing.

5. **Make reconciliation continuous.** Monthly is a cadence designed for a world that assessed you monthly. That world is closing.
