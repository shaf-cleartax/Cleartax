## Background

The company is one of Malaysia's largest digital payments companies. It serves millions of consumers and a wide network of merchants across the country, all running on Oracle Fusion.

In 2024, Malaysia's Inland Revenue Board, LHDN, began rolling out mandatory e-invoicing. Under the Income Tax Act 1967, every business transaction now has to clear the MyInvois platform before it counts as a valid invoice. The rollout is phased by annual turnover, and as a company earning more than RM100 million a year, it sat in the first wave.

The mandate arrived just as the company was expanding from one operating entity to nine. Meeting a brand new requirement at that pace, on a tax team that was not growing, is where the challenge began.

This is the story of how the company plans to meet that mandate with ClearTax, on its existing team, on Oracle, and on the timeline it had set.

## What was the requirement?

The company needed three records to stay in step every single day. The Oracle general ledger, the cleared state on MyInvois, and the tax reporting layer all had to agree at the level of each transaction, across every entity. At this volume, doing that by hand was never an option. Four concerns sat underneath that requirement.

* **More load, same team.** Moving from one entity to nine with no new hires meant carrying nine times the work with the same people. The quiet fear was that something would break and errors would pile up unseen.

* **A first-time mandate.** E-invoicing was new to Malaysia. There was no earlier project to borrow from, and no experience of what an LHDN query looks like or how to answer one.

* **Volume built to grow.** Whatever went in had to hold at 200,000 invoices a month today and stay sound at 6 million a month tomorrow.

* **A hidden classification trap.** Merchant commission earnings are revenue, yet they sit in the accounts payable module. Treat them as purchases and the revenue reported to LHDN is wrong. The fix relied on a clean, consistent chart of accounts across all nine entities.

| Penalties, per offence A missing e-invoice draws a fine of RM200 to RM20,000 per offence under the Income Tax Act 1967\. At this volume, it adds up fast. | Denied tax deductions Expenses without a valid e-invoice can be refused as deductions, and the impact compounds across nine entities. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **No room to catch up by hand** A monthly close cannot keep pace. By month-end, the 72-hour window to fix an error has already closed. | **Brand and regulator trust** As a consumer payments brand, a compliance slip carries weight across the group and its banking ties. |

## Why was Recon AI needed?

Meeting the mandate at this scale is not a monthly filing task. It is a data problem that runs every day, and it needs a product built for that. Recon AI is that product.

Recon AI is ClearTax's reconciliation engine for e-invoicing. It reads a company's own accounting data, compares it against what has actually cleared on MyInvois, and surfaces every gap between the two while there is still time to act.

Every transaction at the company lives in at least two places, the Oracle ledger and MyInvois. At 200,000 invoices a month, those records drift apart unless something checks them continuously. A person cannot keep pace. Recon AI can.

**What Recon AI reads**

Four files flow from the company into Recon AI over a secure connection each day. Together they cover everything the company earns and owes.

* **Accounts payable** carries merchant revenue, commission earned rather than goods bought.

* **General ledger** carries business to consumer transactions.

* **Accounts receivable** carries business to business transactions.

* **Self-billing** carries invoices raised on behalf of suppliers.

GL codes supplied by the company tell the engine which transactions are revenue. From there, Recon AI holds three records in step every day, the Oracle general ledger, the cleared state on MyInvois and the tax reporting layer, all reconciled against one transaction set.
