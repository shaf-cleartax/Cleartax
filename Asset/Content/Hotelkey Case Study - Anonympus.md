**Overview**

The customer is a property management software provider serving hotel properties across Europe. Its platform runs day-to-day hotel operations, from bookings to billing.

From 1 January 2026, Belgium made structured B2B e-invoicing mandatory over the Peppol network. The provider needed compliance built into its own product, as a native feature its hotel customers never have to think about. This is the story of how it delivered exactly that with ClearTax.

**What was the requirement?**

The provider wanted to offer e-invoicing as part of its own platform, under its own brand. That meant carrying real compliance complexity without turning into a compliance company. Its product team is built to run hotels, not to track Peppol specifications.

Several issues sat underneath that goal.

* **No compliance built in.** The property management system had no e-invoicing capability of its own, so there was nothing to switch on for customers facing the mandate.

* **A steep Peppol learning curve.** The network has its own specifications, access point rules and routing behaviour, and the team did not have that expertise in house.

* **Many customers, many VAT structures.** The platform serves multiple hotel customers, each with its own VAT registration and configuration, all needing to be managed cleanly.

* **One standard, more than one country.** Belgium came first, but the same solution had to be ready for other EU countries as their mandates arrive.

* **No central view of delivery.** There was no single place to confirm that every invoice had reached its buyer across all customers.

**The consequences of getting it wrong**

| A gap in the product Without built-in compliance, customers would have to find their own e-invoicing tools, weakening the platform they came for. | Rejected e-invoices Without correct Peppol validation and mapping, invoices risked failing at the receiving access point and never reaching the buyer. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Manual work at scale** Without automation, every customer would fall back on manual steps, adding operational load month after month. | **A slower route to new markets** Without a scalable design, each new EU country would mean building compliance again from the start. |

**How ClearTax helped**

ClearTax embedded its Unified API into the platform, so e-invoicing became a native feature. The provider kept control of onboarding and VAT setup. ClearTax carried the Peppol complexity underneath.

As a registered Peppol Access Point, ClearTax handled every invoice end to end. It mapped the data, validated it against Peppol BIS 3.0, and transmitted it to the buyer. One API covered Belgium and is built to extend across the EU.

When access point issues appeared, ClearTax fixed each one.

* **Validation failures.** Added full XSD validation for BIS 3.0 compliance.

* **Access point compatibility.** Made profile settings dynamic and supported both BIS 3.0 and PINT.

* **Delivery tracking gaps.** Built monitoring to confirm delivery and flag third-party issues.

* **Network migration deadlines.** Guided buyers through the CNAME to NAPTR switch, ahead of time.
