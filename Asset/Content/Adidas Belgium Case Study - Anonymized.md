**Background**

The customer is a leading global sportswear and athletic apparel brand. In Belgium it sells through both physical retail stores and an e-commerce channel, and the two sides of the business run on two independent ERP systems. Together they raise between 1,500 and 2,000 sales invoices a month, all under a single Belgian VAT registration.

From 1 January 2026, Belgium made structured B2B e-invoicing mandatory. It requires PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing for business transactions, with full compliance across all sales channels. Paper and PDF invoices no longer meet the requirement.

For the brand, that meant both the retail and the e-commerce channel had to be PEPPOL-compliant on the same deadline, without slowing down sales. This is the story of how the brand met that mandate with Cleartax, across two ERP systems and on a timeline that left no room to slip.

**What was the requirement?**

The core need was straightforward to describe and demanding to deliver. Every sales invoice from both ERP systems had to be transformed into a PEPPOL-compliant structured e-invoice, validated, and transmitted across the PEPPOL network, for retail and e-commerce alike. Manual uploads were not sustainable at this volume, so both channels needed clean, automated reporting from the first day.

Several issues sat underneath that requirement.

**•   Two systems, one standard.** The retail and e-commerce ERPs were independent, with different data structures, yet both had to meet a single PEPPOL standard.

**•   JSON to UBL mapping.** Both systems produced invoice data in JSON, which had to be transformed precisely into PEPPOL-compliant UBL. Incorrect mapping would mean validation failure.

**•   Buyer identification on PEPPOL.** There was uncertainty around the correct Buyer Participant ID and Scheme ID handling that PEPPOL routing depends on.

**•   Evolving field requirements.** The full set of mandatory fields was not fixed at the outset and kept surfacing during testing.

**•   Staggered timelines.** Retail and e-commerce had different go-live points, so the work had to run on two coordinated but independent tracks.

**How Cleartax helped?**

Cleartax approached the two channels as one compliance problem solved through two independent integrations, rather than forcing both systems into a single mould. It built a unified PEPPOL compliance framework that standardised e-invoice reporting across retail and e-commerce, while respecting each ERP’s own architecture.

At the core was a comprehensive JSON-to-UBL transformation. Cleartax mapped every required invoice element into proper UBL, enforced field validations, absorbed newly introduced fields, and validated every payload against PEPPOL BIS 3.0 before transmission. When retail testing was blocked by Buyer Scheme ID confusion, the team unblocked it quickly with clear guidance on the supported schemes, including 0208 for Belgian enterprise numbers.

Delivery ran on structured project management. Two coordinated tracks, active risk tracking, and iterative UAT support with rapid issue resolution. When new fields surfaced late in testing, Cleartax secured realistic delivery timelines, enabled parallel testing to protect momentum, and folded the fields in before sign-off. The result was a single, reliable reporting flow for both channels.
