**02**

# **The three pillars of readiness**

**Where businesses struggle during implementation projects**

Across our implementations, readiness problems cluster in three areas. The proportions below reflect where enterprises struggle most during live projects. None of these is exotic, and all of them surface only once real invoices start to flow.

| 45% Data quality & mapping *An e-invoice needs granular data in exact places: SIREN, SIRET, address, item codes, VAT and discounts.* One wrong field mapping breaks the invoice downstream, often silently, until a rejection lands. Line-item and GL accuracy decides whether the e-invoice captures the right figure, not just a valid one. |  | 25% Process & business rules *Government-issued codes for state and country need mapping field by field, and this is where master data gaps surface.* Zero-rated and exempt invoices need separate, exact codes, not a catch-all. Every ERP runs its own tax code logic, so a rule that is right in one system is wrong in the next. |  | 30% Master data management *Entity, customer and material or service masters all feed the invoice. If the master is wrong, every invoice built from it is wrong.* Address fields are the usual gap. Country code, department code and postal code are often incomplete. |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

| These percentages are not a scoreboard, they are a warning. The bulk of go-live risk sits in data quality and master records, not in a platform's ability to send a file. A PA that proves only that it can transmit has proved the part that was never in doubt. |
| :---- |

**03**

# **Implementations fail because tax and ERP expertise is rare**

| THE CORE GAP ERP and tax expertise rarely live in one team. For every mapping and every rule decision, both are needed, and in France that means reading SIREN and SIRET structures as fluently as ERP tax tables. The people who know your ERP inside out are seldom the people who know French VAT and DGFiP rules, so the platform is left to bridge a gap it was never built to close. |
