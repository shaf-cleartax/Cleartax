We're navy & off-white with a dash of colour.

Generally speaking, our brand is navy and off-white. Simple and fundamental. Our accent colours are predominantly utilised to highlight information, draw attention to something, or provide an interruption. As a rule of thumb, if your layout starts looking like a rainbow, you've probably gone too far.

In addition to our main brand, primary, and CTA colours, we also have secondary colours. These should only be used for data visualisation and illustrations.

In order to provide more range, particularly for data visualisation, charts, graphs, and illustrations, our colours may be tinted or shaded. These should only be used sparingly. Where possible, the full-strength version of our colours should always be used.

The chart above demonstrates an approximate ratio of our brand colours and how they should be applied. Navy is our primary colour, off-white covers empty space, lime and violet appear in equal measure as accents, blue is held back almost entirely for CTAs, and our secondary colours are used sparingly.

Colour use examples

Here is an example of our brand colours in use. Off-white has the highest percentage, and along with navy, it provides the backbone for the illustration. Grey is used for fill and form, while lime and violet provide interruption in equal measure — drawing attention to the checkmark on the compliance badge and the chart trend line. Naturally, the volume of vibrancy can be turned up or down through colour. If a more subtle or monotone application is required, colour can be removed entirely. If a specific element needs to be highlighted, spot colour can be used. Or, if a more vibrant expression is required, more colour can be added.

We have a very simple rule for applying colour combinations. Don't put colour on colour.

If on a coloured background always use navy or off-white text, and select the one which provides the greatest contrast.

If you're colouring text, it must sit on either an off-white or navy background. Once again, select the one which provides the greatest contrast.

A good deck should look somewhat like this. Generally speaking:

  1. Navy slides for covers, section breakers, and closing slides.
  2. Grey slides for the more technical / administrative sections.
  3. Off-white slides are generally used for the main content.
  4. Colour slides should be used sparingly to highlight key parts of your presentation.

And this is what your deck should not look like.

  1. Try not to overuse colour.
  2. The secondary colours (orange and green) should only be used in graphs, charts, and illustrations. They should not be used for general content.
  3. Blue is reserved for CTAs only — don't use it as a full slide background, even for a highlight.
  4. Our key branded slides, such as covers, closing slides, and breakers should be reserved for navy and off-white — our main brand colours, not lime or violet.
  5. Do not use shades or tints of colours for backgrounds.
